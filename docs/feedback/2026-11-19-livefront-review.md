# Livefront review feedback

Received 2026-11-19 (date shared here), after the design challenge presentation. The role was not offered. Copied verbatim, typos included. Each "area of opportunity" is tracked in [`../BACKLOG.md`](../BACKLOG.md).

## What we liked

- Goals were adeptly identified.
- The serif headings, purple accents, and peace symbol preserve recognizable Craigslist character while giving the interface a more cohesive identity.
- Typography, iconography, colors, and component treatments remain consistent across the presented screens.
- The six category tiles make Craigslist’s broader offering visible without making Home feel exclusively focused on shopping.
- Persistent, labeled navigation keeps Home, Saved, Chat, and Account easy to identify.
- Multiple image sizes, a text-only list, and a map accommodate different browsing preferences.
- Default Material styles were utilized in a way that fits with Craigslist’s brand ethos.
- Bottom sheets, selection controls, and anchored menus demonstrate attention to familiar mobile interaction patterns.
- Filtering was a well-thought-out bonus to the requested screens.
- Filter buttons show the number of matching listings before users apply their selections.
- The listing detail keeps key actions accessible while users scroll through the description and supporting information.
- Considerations for safety and security were balanced with Craigslist’s anonymity.
- Similar listings on the detail screen keep users moving forward.
- Creating a way for sellers to streamline their sale post by starting from a comparable listing was a novel solution.
- The examples include ordinary listing photography and a missing-image treatment, giving a more realistic sense of the interface than polished product imagery alone.
- The timeline slide did a good job of anchoring the user in the work that has been completed.

## Areas of opportunity

- The interface relies heavily on small text, particularly in listing metadata, filter chips, navigation labels, and supporting explanations.
- Large listing images sit alongside compressed titles and metadata, giving photography more space than the information needed to evaluate an item.
- Purple-tinted surfaces appear across navigation, headers, fields, chips, and sheets, weakening the distinction between structural elements and interactive controls.
- The consistently filled Post button competes with the selected navigation destination and makes the active state less immediately clear.
- Placing Post within the navigation bar mixes a creation action with destinations without clearly resolving their different behaviors.
- The floating Save Search button overlaps listing content and gives a secondary action substantial emphasis throughout browsing.
- The small filter chips and tightly packed options make the controls feel less comfortable than the generous rounded styling suggests.
- The search examples omit the keyboard, leaving the available space for suggestions, categories, and listing previews unproven during actual typing.
- Previous and next listing controls sit over the photo alongside photo pagination, making it easy to confuse moving between listings with moving between images.
- Several surface background colors are in play with no clear pattern of usage.
- Hide receives a persistent position beside Share and Message Seller despite being a secondary action.
- Requiring a confirmation sheet to hide a listing adds friction to an action users may repeat while sorting through results.
- The CTAs in silters sit on a surface with no clear boundary, making objects seem cut off.
- “Below typical” badges and price comparisons introduce confident judgments without explaining the data source or how differences in condition, model, and age are accounted for.
- The price chart describes what bikes actually sell for without establishing how completed transaction prices would be available.
- The deck states that research and user testing are complete but does not present findings that substantiate the design decisions.
- Accessibility claims are not accompanied by larger-text layouts or other examples demonstrating how the dense controls and secondary text adapt.
